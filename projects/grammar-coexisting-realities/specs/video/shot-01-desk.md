# 2. WORLD

## World Concept

A study desk, present twice in one frame. On the left the desk as it was in 昭和, with a first-grader seated at it; on the right the same desk now, with the same person at forty seated at it. **Both are present. Neither is the past of the other.** On the desktop lies a single eraser — **the one object that is not drawn twice.**

## World Rules

- **The realities are laid out in one frame and none of them is earlier.** They stay unchanged — nothing passes through the frame, and none of them arrives first.
- **A bearer stands in more than one of them.** The child and the adult are one person — **so the coexistence is visible rather than asserted.**
- **Something is shared across them, so the frame reads as one frame.** ⚠️ **Here it is the eraser, and the light.**
- **⚠️ `SHARED` is exactly one thing.** Every other object on the desk is present twice — **two pencils, two notebooks, two sets of scars in the wood.** The eraser is drawn once.
- **Nothing is cut, dissolved or wiped between the realities, and no line divides them.** They are overlapped, not split.
- **Nothing in the frame declares a year.** No caption, no date stamp, no title card, and no colour grade marking one of them as the past.
- **Neither reality is named as which.** The audience reads the relation from the desk, the features and the eraser — **never from lettering.**
- **⚠️ §15's exemption is this frame's own place and moment, and nothing more.** Identity, the drawing's law, the light's law and the sound hold for the whole shot.

## Visual Language

- Art Direction: **One frame ruled by two media that do not agree.** Everything in it is a **flat two-ink drawing** — a hard pen contour and flat fills, with no named lineage — and **the light alone is photographic**: real falloff, real shadow, real glare laid over the drawn image. The two are neither reconciled nor blended nor feathered. **The drawing owns the contour; the photograph owns the light.**
- Color Language: Each medium keeps its own colour and the two are not reconciled. The drawing's inks are flat; the photographed light has photographic colour. ⚠️ **No grade separates the two realities** — the two rooms are lit the same.
- Texture: Two textures in one frame, meeting at a legible seam. Flat drawn fill on one side of the seam, photographic grain and falloff on the other. ⚠️ **The seam is at the edge of every shadow.**
- Rendering: A drawing whose light is photographed. ⚠️ **Not describable as any existing style** — no named lineage.
- Visual Density: Low. **Two desks overlapped, two figures seated, and one eraser.**
- Time: 昭和といま — **both at once.**
- Atmosphere: Quiet and matter-of-fact. **Nothing here is marvellous to the people in it.**

## Anticipated risks (to check in the first generation)

1. **A second eraser may appear.** ⚠️ **This is the likeliest failure and the most damaging** — a generator given a desk with objects on it will furnish both desks completely. **Then the frame's one shared thing is gone and the shot is a picture of two similar desks.**
2. **A dividing line may be drawn.** Asked for two states side by side, a generator reaches for a split screen. ⚠️ **§2 and §3 require an overlap, and the card forbids the line.**
3. **One reality may be graded as the past.** Sepia, desaturation or a vignette on the 昭和 side is the default move, and it **sequences** the two — **which is the one thing this grammar cannot survive.**
4. **The frame may resolve into one medium.** The seam may be feathered, or the light may settle into flat drawn shading, and then there is no disagreement left and this is no longer the style.
5. **The two figures may read as two people.** If the parting, the eyebrow, the mole and the hand are not drawn identically, the shot becomes a meeting of strangers who resemble each other.
6. **A year may be lettered in.** A caption, a date stamp or a heading on a notebook would name which reality is which — **§2 forbids all lettering in the frame.**
7. **The contour may move.** If the figures drift or the desk shifts, the shot has two moving clocks and the seam stops being legible.
8. ⚠️ **The route's constraints are on the strings, not on the picture.** `docs/h3-route.md` says plainly that no check reads the board and no check can read the drawn picture — **and no board has been drawn here at all.** This specification's §18 is what a board would be drawn from.
