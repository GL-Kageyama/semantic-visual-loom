# Transformation（transformation）

- **Purpose**: Narration (metamorphosis) ／ **Granularity×time**: one subject × unfolding ／ **Size & aspect**: cinematic 16:9, single clip of `DURATION`
- **Summary**: One thing becomes another **without a cut and without a stated process** — the change is the shot, and the viewer watches two states be the same subject at different moments.

## Environment variables
`FROM`＝the substance, figure or place as the shot opens, `TO`＝what it becomes, `TRIGGER`＝the event the change starts from, `KEEP`＝what must stay recognisable across the change, `DURATION`＝clip length

## Composition grammar

⚠️ **This card is `video-spec` plus one grammar.** Fill §1–20 exactly as that card requires; what follows is only what this grammar adds to it.

**The change is the time, and it is not compressed.** This is **unfolding**: the shot spends its seconds on the passage itself, not on the states at either end. A transformation that cuts from A to B is not this grammar — the cut is the one thing the grammar exists to remove.

**No process beat.** The grammar's whole claim is that the change needs no explanation: no intermediate stage held for the viewer, no dissolve marking it, no caption naming it. The two states are the same subject at different moments, and the shot's job is to make that continuity visible rather than asserted.

**One change, one subject.** The subject of the change stays the subject all the way through — that is what `KEEP` is for. A second subject transforming at the same time turns the shot into a montage.

**The return is a decision.** If the shot comes back to the opening state, that return is a second change and must be the shot's own choice, not a drift.

## do
- Spend the shot's seconds on the passage, not on the two states
- State what must stay recognisable, so the change reads as continuity and not as a substitution
- Give the change a trigger, or state that it has none — do not leave the moment it starts to chance
- Let the change finish inside the clip, or state that it is deliberately unfinished

## avoid
- A cut, a dissolve, or a wipe between the two states
- A held intermediate stage that explains the change
- A caption, a voice-over, or a date stamp naming what is happening
- A second subject changing at the same time
- Morphing that abandons the subject's identity instead of carrying it

## Negative
`no cut, no dissolve, no wipe, no stated process, no intermediate stage held, no caption naming the change, no voice-over naming the change, no second simultaneous transformation`
