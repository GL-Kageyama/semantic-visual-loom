# Scale rupture（scale-rupture）

- **Purpose**: Narration (the subject and the environment exchange places) ／ **Granularity×time**: one place × one scale turn ／ **Size & aspect**: cinematic 16:9, single clip of `DURATION`
- **Summary**: **The subject becomes the environment, and the environment becomes the subject.** The shot travels between a macro frame and a wide one until the audience cannot say which was which — and it does not cut to get there.

## Environment variables
`NEAR`＝what fills the frame at the start, `FAR`＝what it turns out to be part of, `TURN`＝the moment the reading flips, `DURATION`＝clip length

## Composition grammar

⚠️ **This card is `video-spec` plus one grammar.** Fill §1–20 exactly as that card requires; what follows is only what this grammar adds to it.

**One continuous travel, no cut.** The rupture happens because the camera moves, not because an edit tells the audience to re-read the picture. **A cut here is a different grammar** — the whole meaning is that the two scales were one image all along.

**The flip must be earned by a detail.** A skin's texture becomes a landscape because a pore reads as a crater; a crowd becomes a pattern because a shoulder reads as a ridge. **Name the shared detail in §4, and keep it visible through the turn.**

**The turn is one-directional.** Up or down — **not both in one shot.** A shot that goes in and comes back out is a zoom; a shot that goes in and finds the world on the other side is this card.

**Distinguish from `impossible-camera`.** That card is about a viewpoint no body can have. **This one is about a viewpoint any body can have, arriving somewhere it did not expect.**

## do
- Name the detail that reads at both scales, and hold it in frame through the turn
- State the direction of travel in §10 and keep it
- Let the turn be caused by the camera's motion, not by a focus pull alone
- End on the far scale **already in motion** — the world continues past the arrival

## avoid
- A cut at the turn, or a dissolve standing in for the travel
- A focus rack alone, with the camera standing still
- A turn that happens twice in one shot
- A CGI "zoom into the eye" with no detail carrying across

## Negative
`no cut at the turn, no dissolve standing in for the travel, no focus rack alone, no turn that happens twice, no zoom into an eye without a shared detail, no on-screen text naming the scale, no spiral transition`

## When it earns its place
- **The value it turns.** Small becomes large, or inside becomes outside — **and the audience's sense of what matters flips with it.**
- **What the audience loses without it.** The vertigo of a world that was always bigger or smaller than it looked. **A cut can say "meanwhile"; only travel can say "it was this all along".**
- **The grounding shot.** A shot of the same world with an ordinary camera, before or after — **so the rupture is measured against a scale that stayed put.**
- **What it costs.** The shot's whole seconds and one direction. **The budget is the travel itself.**

## Failure it is confused with
- ⚠️ **An ordinary zoom is not a rupture.** If the subject and the environment do not exchange roles, the camera has only moved.
- **A macro insert followed by a wide shot.** Two shots are not a turn.
- **A trick shot.** If the shared detail is not visible at both scales, the audience sees a cut disguised as a move.
